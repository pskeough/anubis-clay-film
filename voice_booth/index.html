    Mic not working in Brave? (opens by itself when something fails)
    1. Use the address http://127.0.0.1:7090 that VoiceBooth.bat opens, not a file you double-clicked. The mic only works on a local server address.
    2. Click the icon at the left end of the address bar, then Site settings (or the mic entry), and set Microphone: Allow. Once a site has been blocked, Brave stops asking, so this is the usual fix. Reload after changing it.
    3. Open brave://settings/content/microphone: pick the right default device at the top, and remove 127.0.0.1:7090 from "Not allowed to use your microphone" if it is listed.
    4. If the device list is empty or the meter stays flat with permission granted, lower Shields for this site (lion icon, Shields off). Fingerprinting protection can hide or blank devices.
    5. Windows: Settings → Privacy & security → Microphone: "Microphone access" and "Let desktop apps access your microphone" both on.
    6. "Device in use" means another app holds the mic exclusively (Discord, OBS, a DAW). Close it, or turn off exclusive mode in Sound settings → the mic → Advanced.
    7. Still stuck: paste the same address into Edge or Chrome. Everything works the same and takes save to the same folder.
    Sounding better
    • Distance first. A hand-width to two fists from the grille. Half the distance is 6 dB more voice over the same room. Then set the gain so your loudest words peak around −10 on the meter.
    • Talk across it, not into it. Aim the mic at your chin or angle it 30° off your mouth: the pops and breath blasts go past it.
    • Room. Soft things close behind you and around the mic (a duvet over a chair back works). Turn off fans; point the back of the mic at the PC.
    • Headphones on, guide off while recording, or at least low.
    • Slow the piece down until you can land each line with room to breathe, then record shot by shot. A relaxed read at 0.8 beats a sprint at 1.0.
    • Water, not coffee, a few minutes before; mouth clicks come from a dry mouth.
    • Close heavy apps while recording. A busy CPU is what makes the browser drop samples.
    • The build's broadcast polish does the rest: denoise from your count-in room tone, rumble and mud cut, presence, de-ess, gentle compression, loudness to −16 LUFS. The AI voice has all of that baked in, which is most of why it sounds bigger.
